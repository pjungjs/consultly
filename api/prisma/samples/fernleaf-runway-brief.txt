# Fernleaf: runway and Series A planning brief
Prepared for: session with Kenji Watanabe (startup finance)
Company: Fernleaf, an ordering and subscription platform for independent bakeries

# Where we are
Fernleaf lets neighbourhood bakeries sell weekly bread and pastry subscriptions online. We launched 22 months ago and raised a $1.2M seed round 11 months ago. We are a team of 7: two founders, three engineers, one customer success lead and one part-time designer.

- 310 paying bakeries, mostly in the Pacific Northwest
- $420k annual recurring revenue, growing about 6% month over month for the last two quarters
- Monthly logo churn of 2.8%
- Gross margin around 78% after payment processing and hosting
- Blended customer acquisition cost of roughly $640 per bakery
- Net burn of about $85k per month; cash in the bank $1.19M as of October 1

# What we are deciding
We want to raise a Series A in Q2 2027. Before that we have two decisions:
1. Whether to hire two more engineers now (about $32k per month fully loaded) or wait until we pass $600k ARR.
2. Whether to raise our main plan from $79 to $129 per month for new bakeries. Our largest customers have told us they would pay more for multi-location support, which ships in December.

# What we want from the session
- Pressure-test our runway model (spreadsheet attached separately) and tell us what investors will look at first.
- Help us frame the price increase: how to model its effect on churn and payback.
- Tell us which unit economics we should lead with in the Series A deck.

# Constraints
Our lead seed investor wants a board update with a hiring plan by November 20. We do not want to raise a bridge round.
